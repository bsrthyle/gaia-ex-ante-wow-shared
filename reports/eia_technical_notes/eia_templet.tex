\section*{Soil Acidity and Its Impacts on Agriculture}
Soil acidity significantly reduces the productivity of agricultural land by limiting nutrient availability and impairing root growth. In \CountryName, soil acidity is a major constraint on crop production, particularly in key agricultural regions. Acidic soils result in poor crop establishment, reduced nutrient uptake, and ultimately lower yields. Addressing soil acidity through lime application can help alleviate these constraints and improve crop health, leading to more resilient agricultural systems.

Recent analysis of soils in \CountryName shows that many areas with low pH levels are characterized by high exchangeable acidity, which impedes root growth and nutrient uptake by crops. Exchangeable acidity can be most effectively addressed through lime application. However, in regions where acidity is low, other approaches such as integrated soil fertility management (ISFM) that combine mineral and organic fertilizer inputs may be more cost-effective.

Exchangeable acidity, expressed as a percentage of the Effective Cation Exchange Capacity (ECEC), indicates the need for targeted lime interventions to restore soil productivity in these areas. Prioritizing regions with exchangeable acidity above 10\% of ECEC is recommended, as these areas are more likely to experience severe yield losses due to acidity.

\section*{Implementation Considerations}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Cost and Logistics}: The logistics of lime distribution and application are critical to the success of soil remediation efforts. In \CountryName, challenges such as limited infrastructure, high transportation costs, and inadequate supply chains may hinder lime availability in remote areas. Effective planning and coordination among government agencies, private sector stakeholders, and farmer cooperatives are essential to overcome these challenges.
\item \textbf{Farmer Awareness and Training}: Increasing farmer awareness of soil acidity and its impact on crop yields is crucial. Extension services and training programs should be established to educate farmers on the benefits of lime application, proper application rates, and best practices for improving soil health. Demonstration plots and farmer field schools can be used to showcase the positive effects of lime on crop performance.
\item \textbf{Policy Support}: Government policies that promote lime use through subsidies, credit facilities, and incentives can help reduce the financial burden on farmers and encourage adoption. Policymakers should prioritize the development of supportive policies that facilitate lime production, distribution, and application in high-priority areas.
\item \textbf{Targeted Promotion}: Recommendations for targeting lime distribution include ramping up soil testing efforts and focusing on areas with high exchangeable acidity (> 10\%). Targeting specific crops, such as legumes and root and tuber crops, may also yield greater returns compared to cereals, given their different sensitivities to soil acidity.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Key Assumptions and Parameters}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Acidic Cropland Definition}: Cropland with acidity saturation levels above 10\% of ECEC is classified as acidic.
\item \textbf{Yield Response}: Crop yields are assumed to be unaffected by soil acidity until a critical acidity threshold, known as the Target Acidity Saturation (TAS), is reached. Beyond this point, yields decrease linearly, with zero yield expected at very high acidity levels.
\item \textbf{Economic Benefit Calculation}: The benefit is calculated as the additional yield from liming multiplied by the median crop price, while costs are determined by multiplying the lime rate by a fixed price of 100 USD/tonne.
\item \textbf{Timeframe}: The benefits of lime application are assessed for the year of application due to limited data on the long-term effects of liming in sub-Saharan Africa. Further research is needed to evaluate the residual benefits of lime over multiple growing seasons.
\item \textbf{Crop Differences}: Different crops have varying tolerances to soil acidity, which influences lime recommendations. For cereals, 70\% of cropland may not require lime, while for legumes, up to 45\% of cropland could benefit from lime application.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Conclusion}
Targeting lime application in the most affected regions of \CountryName presents an opportunity to significantly increase agricultural productivity and improve food security. By addressing soil acidity, policymakers can prioritize interventions that yield the highest economic returns and ensure long-term agricultural sustainability. In addition, lime application can contribute to building resilient farming systems capable of withstanding the challenges of climate variability and soil degradation. Given the substantial logistics involved in lime distribution, it is important to focus on regions where the return on investment is highest, and consider alternative soil fertility management practices where appropriate.
